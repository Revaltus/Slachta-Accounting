## Accounting built around how dental practices run

Dental practices run on a different clock than most small businesses. Scheduling drives revenue, insurance reimbursements lag behind the work, and hygienists, associates, and front-desk staff are not all paid the same way.

Production, collections ratios, hygiene department performance, lab fees, and staff-to-chair ratios tell a different story than a standard income statement. A CPA needs to know the difference between a slow month and a real overhead problem. Slachta Accounting & Consulting Services LLC was built on referrals from dentists and specialty healthcare owners who wanted exactly that, and we handle the accounting, payroll, and tax work around the rhythms of a dental office.

<!-- block: checklist-section | variant: standalone -->
## Where dental practice finances get stuck

- **Cash that trails the work.** Insurance payments arrive weeks after treatment and patient financing plans stretch revenue over months. A busy schedule can still leave the account thin.
- **Payroll with many moving parts.** Hourly hygienists, associates paid on production, and salaried managers all need different handling, plus tax deposits and filings that keep pace with turnover.
- **Tax bills that surface too late.** Most dentists learn what they owe after the year closes, when equipment purchases and retirement contributions can no longer change the result.
- **Retirement and growth decisions made on stale numbers.** Choosing a plan, adding an associate, or opening a second location needs current figures, not records months out of date.

## Common questions from dentists

**Q: Do you work with dentists outside Minnesota and Wisconsin?**
A: Yes. Our dental client base is concentrated in Minnesota and Wisconsin, but we serve dental practice owners nationwide with outsourced accounting, payroll, and tax planning delivered remotely, with the same monthly reporting and quarterly check-ins.

**Q: What is included in outsourced accounting for a dental practice?**
A: Monthly bookkeeping, bank and merchant account reconciliation, and production-versus-collections tracking through a secure platform. Expenses are tied to dental categories like lab fees, supplies, and equipment. Statements arrive on a set schedule, so you see how the practice is performing now, not months later.

**Q: What does dental office payroll processing cover?**
A: It covers hourly hygienists, associates paid on production percentages, salaried staff, tax deposits, and quarterly and annual filings. It also includes benefits and PTO tracking, plus checks for overtime and benefits cost creep through staff changes, leave, hiring, and associate buy-ins.

**Q: How is dental tax planning different from year-end tax prep?**
A: Tax prep reports what already happened. Planning runs through the year on current practice data, evaluating entity structure, equipment purchases, Section 179 elections, and retirement contribution timing before December, while you can still adjust estimated payments or purchase timing.

**Q: What practice structures do you prepare returns for?**
A: We prepare business returns for dental practices structured as S corporations, partnerships, and professional corporations. We coordinate them with the owner's individual return, since practice income, retirement contributions, and personal deductions rarely sit apart for a dentist.

**Q: Can I get CFO-level guidance without hiring a full-time CFO?**
A: Yes. Outsourced CFO and controller support includes KPI tracking for production, collections, overhead percentage, and new patients, along with budgeting around seasonal volume and equipment replacement, and cash flow forecasting that reflects insurance lag. It suits practices not yet large enough for a CFO on payroll.

**Q: Will you coordinate with my financial advisor on retirement plans?**
A: Yes. We work directly with your advisor on retirement plan evaluation, whether a SIMPLE IRA, SEP, or 401(k) with profit sharing, so the design fits your entity structure, cash flow, and personal financial planning. Both of us work from the same current financial picture.

**Q: Can you help with adding an associate, a second location, or a sale?**
A: Yes. We model what an associate's compensation does to overhead, what a second location does to first-year cash flow, and what the practice is worth before a transition conversation starts. We have worked with dental and specialty healthcare clients since 2005, through more than one growth stage.
