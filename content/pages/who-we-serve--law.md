## Accounting that understands trust accounts, retainers, and partner draws

You bill by the matter, collect on someone else's timeline, and answer to the state bar for every client dollar you hold. Most accountants treat that like any other small business. We don't.

Slachta Accounting & Consulting Services has worked with attorneys since 2005, founded in Stillwater, Minnesota. We serve law practices across Minnesota and Wisconsin, and nationwide, so partners can see where the firm stands without waiting for a year-end summary.

## Financial pressure points in running a law practice

- **Trust accounting.** Client funds must stay strictly separate from operating funds, and reconciliation can't slip. One missed month creates a compliance problem.
- **Uneven cash flow.** Revenue rises and falls with case timelines, contingency settlements, and retainer billing cycles. Planning around that takes real numbers.
- **Partner compensation.** Origination credit, seniority tiers, and profit-sharing formulas make monthly reporting harder than a standard template allows.
- **Expense visibility.** Matters, support staff, and overhead need to be visible to every partner, not only the managing partner.

## Common questions from attorneys

**Q: Does Slachta handle trust accounting for law firms?**
A: Yes. Trust accounting oversight is part of outsourced bookkeeping for legal clients, including reconciliation and strict separation of client funds from operating accounts. A secure platform gives attorneys visibility into trust account activity, which supports compliance with state bar rules without a manual audit at month-end. Problems surface immediately rather than in a year-end review.

**Q: Can Slachta manage payroll for a small law firm with just a few employees?**
A: Yes. Payroll scales from a solo attorney with one assistant to firms with dozens of employees, handling salaried associates, hourly paralegals, and partner draws with on-time processing. Because payroll connects to the firm's accounting, labor costs are visible next to other financial data, which supports better staffing and overhead decisions.

**Q: How does entity structure affect a law firm's taxes?**
A: Whether a firm is an LLC, partnership, or professional corporation changes how partner distributions are taxed and which retirement and deduction options are available. Tax planning starts by reviewing that structure before filing season, using current data to model the year ahead. Growth, new partners, or a second location often change the right choice.

**Q: What does modern accounting look like for a law firm?**
A: It rests on four pillars: financial visibility so partners know where the firm stands, connected technology that keeps bookkeeping, payroll, and tax data aligned without re-entry, proactive guidance that arrives before problems develop, and data-driven insights that ground every cash flow, budgeting, or tax conversation in current numbers.

**Q: Does Slachta work with law firms outside Minnesota and Wisconsin?**
A: Yes. The firm is based in Stillwater, Minnesota, with a concentration of clients in Minnesota and Wisconsin, and serves attorneys nationwide through remote bookkeeping, payroll, and tax services. A secure platform keeps real-time visibility available wherever the firm is located.

**Q: What is matter-based expense tracking?**
A: It means costs trace back to the case or client they support instead of sitting in a general ledger. Partners can see which matters carry which expenses, and cash flow projections can account for the gap between billing a matter and collecting on it. This is the same discipline we apply to service-based businesses.

**Q: How does Slachta help with partner retirement plans?**
A: Retirement plan evaluation is a regular part of advisory work. We check plan design against the firm's current partner mix, compare contribution limits with what each partner wants to save, and stay in contact with each partner's outside financial advisor so numbers line up across connected plans. This suits partners at different career stages.

**Q: Do you work with industries other than law?**
A: Yes. Dentists, architects, insurance agencies, specialty healthcare services, and established small businesses bring similar expense management and cash flow concerns. Law practices add compliance weight, such as trust accounting and partner compensation, and we plan for that. You can browse the other industries we serve on our industries page.
