## Your practice runs on reimbursements, payroll, and patient care

If you run an oral surgery, occupational therapy, speech therapy, or chiropractic practice, you manage two businesses at once: the clinical work and everything behind it. Insurance reimbursements from commercial carriers, Medicare, and Medicaid arrive on their own schedule. Clinical payroll goes out on time regardless.

Your books can show strong production and thin cash in the same month. That is normal for this field, and it is something we know how to read.

Slachta Accounting & Consulting Services LLC has worked with specialty healthcare practice owners in Minnesota, Wisconsin, and nationwide since 2005. We handle the financial side so you can stay focused on patients.

<!-- block: checklist-section | variant: standalone -->
## Where specialty practices lose visibility

- **Reimbursement lag.** Payments can trail the service date by 30, 60, or even 90 days. Production looks healthy while the bank balance says otherwise.
- **Staffing costs.** Clinical staff on varied pay structures make payroll hard to forecast. Staff cost ratios shift with patient load.
- **Mixed revenue streams.** Therapists and chiropractors balance insurance contracts with cash-pay services. It is hard to tell which streams actually perform.
- **Heavy overhead.** Specialized equipment and facility costs stay fixed in a slow month. Without clear budgets and KPIs, it is tough to know what predicts a good or bad month.

## Common questions from specialty healthcare providers

**Q: Do you work with dental practices as well as specialty healthcare practices?**
A: Yes. Dentists are one of our core niches, alongside specialty healthcare services and architects. We handle entity structuring, payroll for hygienists and associates, and reimbursement-driven cash flow planning. Practice owners get visibility into their numbers so decisions are made on current data rather than a year-end summary.

**Q: How do you handle accounting for insurance reimbursement delays?**
A: We build reporting that separates production from actual collections, so you see the gap between billed services and cash in the bank at any point in the month. Reimbursements from commercial carriers, Medicare, and Medicaid can lag 30, 60, or 90 days, so this view supports budgeting, staffing, and quarterly tax estimates.

**Q: What financial issues are specific to oral surgeons, therapists, and chiropractors?**
A: Oral surgeons face equipment depreciation and surgical facility fee complexity. Occupational and speech therapists often work across private practice, school contracts, and home health, each with different billing and revenue recognition rules. Chiropractors handle high visit volume, mixed insurance and cash-pay revenue, and staffing ratios that shift with patient load.

**Q: Which KPIs should a specialty healthcare practice track?**
A: We focus on collection rate, provider utilization, overhead as a percentage of revenue, and staff cost ratios. Together they show whether production is turning into cash and whether costs are in line. We pair them with budgets built on your real overhead to guide staffing, pricing, and growth decisions.

**Q: Can you help with entity structure decisions for a growing practice?**
A: Yes. We work through S corp, PLLC, and partnership options as practices add providers, locations, or buy-in partners. We factor in tax impact, retirement plan contributions, and ownership transition timing. We also plan depreciation for surgical and therapy equipment and facility improvements, and coordinate quarterly estimated taxes around uneven reimbursement income.

**Q: What retirement plan options do you evaluate for practice owners?**
A: We evaluate SEP, SIMPLE, and 401(k) options for practices with both owner-providers and support staff. The right choice depends on your entity, staffing, and income pattern. We also coordinate with your financial advisor so personal tax and retirement decisions line up with the practice's plans.

**Q: What compliance and reporting work do you handle?**
A: We handle the accounting side of payroll tax filings, 1099 reporting for contracted providers, and state-specific reporting needs. We also prepare financial statements and supporting documentation for bank reviews, partner buy-in valuations, and internal audits, and we coordinate directly with outside auditors or lenders on your behalf.

**Q: Do you work with healthcare practices outside Minnesota?**
A: Yes. We serve clients nationwide, with the heaviest concentration in Minnesota and Wisconsin. Our firm was founded in 2005 in Stillwater, Minnesota, and our outsourced accounting and payroll services are delivered through technology that gives you the same visibility into your numbers wherever your practice is located.
